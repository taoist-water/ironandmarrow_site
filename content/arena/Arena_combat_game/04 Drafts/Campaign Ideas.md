# Campaign Ideas (draft)

Oct 27, 2026 · @TheTao · *Ideas to explore later. Nothing here is a rule, and none of it is scheduled.*

## Purpose and guardrails

The campaign is an optional add-on. A one-off game must stay exactly as it is. Any campaign idea has to pass these tests:

- **One-off play is untouched.** No campaign rule changes a single game unless both players opt in.
- **Games stay fast.** A campaign game still targets 75 minutes or less at four players.
- **Bookkeeping stays light.** Between games, each player spends 10 minutes or less updating their warband.
- **The four pillars still apply,** especially pillar 4: a campaign must not let early winners run away with it.
- **Sources are named.** Most of these ideas come from Mordheim's campaign, which is the main reference.

## Setting frame: the Circuit

An arena game suits a travelling blood-sport circuit better than ruins to explore. Warbands fight in a run of fighting pits, each with its own arena layout and hazards, for patrons, coin and renown. A campaign is a season on the Circuit.

- Each venue is a new arena layout, using the hex board's still-undesigned terrain (obstructions, pits).
- A season is roughly 5–8 games, with a final showdown.
- Players can drop in or miss a night without breaking the season.

## Progression

- **Renown (experience).** Fighters earn Renown from Glory they score, taking out enemies and surviving. *Source: Mordheim experience.*
- **Advancement as traits.** Renown buys new traits; the natural reward is raising a veteran fighter's trait cap from 2 to 3. Traits are already priced, so advancement costs are known.
- **Promotion.** A fighter who survives long enough changes chassis, for example Skirmisher to Duelist.
- **Leader titles.** A Leader who wins a venue gains a one-line title ability.
- **Creed advancement.** Each Creed advances its own way: Rift Cult fighters mutate, the Unburied raise the fallen, the Penitent earn relics.

## Injury and death

- **Lasting Critical Injuries.** The Critical Injury cards already stay with a fighter for the rest of a game; in a campaign some stay for the season.
- **Post-game injury roll** for every fighter taken out: fine, injured, crippled or dead. *Source: Mordheim's serious injury table.*
- **Death and replacement.** A dead fighter's traits are lost; the warband hires a new fighter with coin.
- **Retirement.** A fighter with too many injuries can be retired for a one-off reward instead of dying.

## Economy

- **Coin from Glory.** Glory scored converts to coin after each game.
- **Hiring and upkeep.** Coin hires new fighters and replaces losses. Upkeep stops warbands from hoarding.
- **The prize.** The Circuit's equivalent of Mordheim's wyrdstone: a contested resource fought over in some venues.
- **Warband rating** = total points plus advancement. It is used for underdog bonuses. *Source: Mordheim warband rating.*

## Keeping it fair across games

These are the most important ideas here, because campaigns snowball.

- **Underdog bonus.** The lower-rated warband earns extra Renown or coin, scaled by the rating gap. *Source: Mordheim underdog experience.*
- **Points cap per game.** Every warband fields 60 points (or the season's current cap) whatever its rating, so experience buys variety, not raw power.
- **Catch-up between games.** The last-placed warband after each game picks the next venue or gets first choice of a hire.
- **Rivalries.** A lasting version of the Bounty: whoever took out your Leader becomes a rival worth extra Renown. Watch for dogpiling across a whole season.

## Patrons and events

- **Patrons.** Each warband has a patron with its own goal for the season. Hidden goals risk kingmaking, so patrons' goals should be public.
- **Between-game events.** A small deck of events between games: a plague, a bribe, a debt called in.
- **Venue rules.** Each venue adds one rule: a flooded pit, a crowd that throws weapons, a blood-price for retreating.

## Hooks to protect in the core rules now

The core game doesn't need any of this yet, but these choices keep a campaign possible:

- **Points as currency.** Chassis and traits keep exact point costs, so advancement and hiring can be priced.
- **Traits as advancement.** Keep traits one line each and self-contained, so adding a third is simple.
- **Lasting effects on cards.** Critical Injuries stay physical cards that can travel with a fighter.
- **Fighter cards with room.** A campaign version of the fighter card needs space for Renown and injuries.
- **No rule that assumes a fresh warband,** such as a rule that depends on every fighter starting at full Wounds every game.

## Open questions

1. Season length: a fixed number of games, or until someone hits a Renown target?
2. Does a warband ever grow above 4 fighters (5 for Gutter Kin)?
3. How much can a veteran warband outclass a new one before the underdog rules stop working?
4. Is the Circuit the right frame, or does the campaign leave the arena for the world outside?
